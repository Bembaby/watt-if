You know that moment when the evening changes... but your to-do list doesn't? That's the idea behind Watt If. Three flexible chores, a few boundaries, and a plan you can actually look at before saying yes. This is an Alexa Plus simulation. Let's walk through it.

First, dryer and dishwasher by eight. Bike charged by ten. I check the times... approve... and download.

Now, dryer at nine. Quiet hours at eight. That doesn't fit. Good. It refuses the request.

Let's make room. Dryer finished by midnight; quiet after ten. It keeps the nine o'clock start. I review... approve... and download again.

Here's what that last click gives us: a real calendar file. The dryer goes from nine to ten. The dishwasher and bike have their own slots. These are fictional household details, but the download is real. And there's an important boundary here. Approving a timetable only creates advisory calendar events. It doesn't turn anything on. You still decide what happens in your home.

The interesting part is what happens between asking and approving. Strands runs the language model. The planning tool checks the changes against the request and the household rules. If the constraints clash, there's no fresh plan to approve. A correction creates a new revision, so yesterday's yes can't quietly become permission for something different. The evidence includes the requests, both approvals, and the files that actually downloaded.

This recording used Qwen on a temporary GPU, through a private SSH connection. The app can also use local Ollama, or its manual controls without a model. Fifty-eight regression tests pass, alongside a separate solver check. Those are bounded checks, not a promise that every sentence will work. No live Alexa connection. No appliance control. No claimed energy savings. Just a little more room to see your choices... before you make them.
